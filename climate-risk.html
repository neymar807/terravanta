Method: NASA POWER monthly point data for T2M and PRECTOTCORR. For each calendar month, the 2025 monthly value is compared with the mean of that same month across 1991–2020. Temperature departure is in °C. Precipitation departure is percentage difference in monthly mean daily precipitation rate (mm/day), not annual rainfall total. A missing month is left unavailable rather than estimated. This is a climate comparison, not proof of climate-change attribution or a farm-level impact assessment.

Data interpretationThese are NASA POWER gridded/reanalysis climate observations for the selected coordinates, not measurements taken on a particular farm. The 35°C hot-day count is a simple descriptive threshold and is not a crop-specific heat-stress diagnosis. Precipitation does not measure irrigation access, groundwater, soil moisture, or drought impacts.

How to read these observations

  • Mean temperature is the average of valid daily T2M values.
  • Hot-day count is the number of daily T2M_MAX values at or above 35°C; it is not a crop-specific stress threshold.
  • Annual precipitation is the sum of valid daily PRECTOTCORR values.
  • Rainfall variability is the coefficient of variation of the 12 monthly precipitation totals.

What NASA POWER cannot tell us alone

  • Actual soil moisture or soil condition on a particular farm.
  • Whether a farmer can access irrigation or groundwater.
  • Crop variety, planting date or growth-stage sensitivity.
  • Farm-level yield impacts or a planting recommendation.
NASA data source and methodTerraVanta requests daily T2M, T2M_MAX and PRECTOTCORR from NASA POWER for 1 January–31 December 2025. The page calculates the mean of available daily T2M values, counts days with T2M_MAX ≥ 35°C, sums daily PRECTOTCORR, and calculates monthly rainfall totals and their coefficient of variation. NASA POWER documents daily point time-series data and availability from 1981 to near real time. Missing-value codes are excluded. NASA POWER Daily API documentation.
