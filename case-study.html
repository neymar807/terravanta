How to interpret this comparisonCompare the direction and size of changes within each region. Temperature is the mean of valid daily T2M values; precipitation is the sum of valid daily PRECTOTCORR values. These are gridded regional estimates, not farm measurements. Differences between regions do not by themselves prove climate-change attribution or crop impacts.

How to investigate the results

  1. Compare each region with its own earlier years to identify direction and consistency of change.
  2. Compare rainfall totals and note that annual totals can hide seasonal timing and dry spells.
  3. Check whether a year has fewer valid daily records before interpreting its values.
  4. Use the satellite vegetation page to explore broad vegetation patterns, while considering seasonality and cloud/quality limitations.

What this case study can and cannot conclude

It can show: regional gridded estimates of mean daily air temperature and summed daily corrected precipitation for the selected grid locations and years.

It cannot prove: the cause of a trend, crop yield changes, farm-level drought, soil moisture, irrigation demand, or which crop a farmer should plant. Those questions need additional observations, crop calendars, soil information and local expertise.
